About me

From thermal engineering to molecular biophysics

I am Athul Vidya Rajeev, a PhD research scholar at the Indian Institute of Technology Hyderabad, working with Dr. Harikrishnan N. Unni. My thesis, “Multiscale Computational Modelling of Nanoparticle Transport and Interfacial Interactions for Targeted Drug Delivery”, asks how surface chemistry and membrane composition decide whether a nanoparticle enters a healthy or a cancer cell.

At the molecular scale I run all-atom MD simulations in NAMD and GROMACS and compute free-energy profiles with adaptive biasing force and umbrella sampling, paying close attention to sampling and convergence. At the device scale I model flow and particle transport in microfluidic channels with COMSOL Multiphysics, and I have worked with experimental colleagues on microfluidic chips that led to two granted Indian patents.

I trained as a mechanical engineer (B.Tech, and M.Tech in Thermal Science), and I enjoy teaching thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, heat transfer and CFD.

Research interests

What I work on

01

Enhanced sampling & free energy

Steered MD to build pathways, then stratified multi-window adaptive biasing force and umbrella sampling to compute potentials of mean force, with systematic checks of sampling and convergence.

NAMD · Colvars · GROMACS · VMD · Python
02

Nanoparticle–membrane interactions

How bare and PEG-functionalised gold nanoparticles bind to and cross normal and cancer-mimicking lipid bilayers, and how coating and lipid composition change the energetics.

Lipid bilayers · membrane deformation · water pores · order parameters
03

Membrane proteins

Nanoparticle transport across membranes containing the TRPV1 ion channel, and how a protein-embedded membrane changes contacts and transport energetics.

TRPV1 · protein–membrane systems · contact analysis
04

Microfluidics & continuum transport

Laminar flow, drag, Brownian motion and particle tracing in microscale geometries, linking device-scale transport with molecular-scale energetics.

COMSOL Multiphysics · ANSYS Fluent · microfluidic chip fabrication
